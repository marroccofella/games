Other-agent groundwork, reviewed and brought across

The supplied recap described a separate development version. These generic improvements are now integrated into this public release:

  • Jointed knees and elbows, faces, hair variations, outfits, fabric colour variation, idle breathing, panic poses and zombie gait. Turned guests retain their clothes.
  • A smaller body collision radius, sliding at corners and door jambs, guest give-way behaviour and a wider recruitment radius.
  • Doors folded against the adjoining wall, clearer EXIT signs and smaller furniture kept away from doorway approaches.
  • Top, Shoulder and Eyes camera modes; close-view aiming, mouse look, quick turn and a zoomed, rotating minimap.
  • Walkable rescue trails, objective distance and map trails: green for the coach, amber for a grabbed guest, pale for another guest. With followers, the coach takes priority.

The recap also reported performance timings and full-night rescue totals. Those numbers were not reproduced for this release and are not offered as verified benchmarks. Its real-site layout changes are outside the fictional public game's scope.

Improvements in this release

  • Sound: rewritten WebAudio engine, camera-relative stereo, distance attenuation, wall muffling, indoor reverberation, layered weapon/mechanical impacts, creature vocalisations and footsteps. These are synthesised sounds, with no recorded or AI-generated speech.
  • Suspense: low drones, nearby-threat heartbeat, breathing, encounter stinger and a visible danger cue; quieter music leaves room for threats. A Quiet mix is available.
  • Presentation: textured plaster, a damp-looking road surface, restrained rain, cool face fill against warm street lamps, subtle shoulder-lens tension, reduced first-person bob and torch power that falls near walls.
  • Combat: denser blood spray, irregular wet-looking floor splashes, a bounded debris pool, varied falling poses and limb reactions. Full or Reduced gore is selectable.
  • Comfort: reduced camera motion, steady lamps, softer muzzle flashes, optional guide trail, persistent local presentation preferences, and a sound-check button in Pause.
  • Reliability: 28-voice cap and audio graph cleanup; geometry disposal when people leave or a game restarts; route marks follow cardinal grid edges; snag recovery cannot jump to a distant cell or cross a blocked line.
  • Monty: the lavender suit, gold briefcase and negotiations remain. His briefcase now fits a human hand; the valuation still does not.

Verification and limits

The release includes 21 gameplay checks and 10 audio graph/routing checks. Gameplay checks cover all 13 rooms, 17 doors, actual simulated movement from every room to the coach, pause/resume, touch resize, rescue counting, camera modes, presentation controls and Monty. Audio checks cover direction, walls, distance, lifecycle cleanup, voice limits and resume timing.

Local browser checks exercised rendering, movement, firing, all three views and the sound check, which reported a running engine with zero recorded audio errors. The scripted tests use a stub renderer or simulated audio graph; an engine status is not proof of speaker playback or subjective sound quality. No universal frame-rate claim is made.

Lighting, faces and gore remain procedural and stylised. No physics ragdolls, recorded voice acting, full-body motion capture, ray tracing or accurate acoustic simulation are claimed. The public hotel and its map remain invented. This is a standalone game, not a model of a real building.
